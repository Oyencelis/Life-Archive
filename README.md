# 🗄️ Archive

### *A private, single-owner life archive.*

A place to keep the people, places, memories, projects, and small moments that made you — searchable, connected, and never built to be seen by anyone but you.

</div>

<br>

> This isn't a journaling app, a social network, or a photo backup tool — though it does pieces of all three. It's closer to a personal museum: every person you've known, every place that mattered, every memory worth keeping, every goal you chased or abandoned, every thing you made — recorded once, connected to everything else it touches, and kept private by default.

**There is no sign-up, no feed, no followers, and no algorithm.** The only account this archive will ever have is yours.

## 💭 The idea behind it

Most places you'd put this kind of thing — social media, a notes app, scattered photo albums — either weren't built to be private, weren't built to last, or weren't built to connect. A memory doesn't just have a date; it has people in it, a place it happened, and a chapter of your life it belongs to. This archive treats all of that as **one connected graph** instead of a pile of disconnected entries, so a person you add automatically becomes something you can trace through every memory, journal entry, and event they show up in.

Nothing here is designed to be performed for an audience. Journal entries are private by default. There's no "public profile." The one thing resembling a social feature — a connections graph — is centered entirely on *you*, because that's the only account that exists.

## 🚪 Getting started

| | |
|---|---|
| **First run** | The very first time the archive is opened, it asks you to create the one owner account it will ever have — your name, email, and a password. There's no invite system and no second account; this is a single-owner archive, permanently. |
| **Signing in** | After that, every visit starts at a sign-in screen. Your session stays open until you sign out. |
| **Setting the mood** | Head to **Settings → Atmospheres** early on and pick a visual theme — *Minimal, Midnight, Cyber, Cute, Editorial, Archive, Retro/Pixel, Y2K, Organic, Futuristic,* or *Terminal* — or build your own from scratch with the custom theme editor. The theme you pick follows you everywhere, including the sign-in screen itself. |

## 🗃️ What you can keep here

### 📸 Memories
Titled, dated moments — the core unit of the archive. Each one can carry a description, a mood, an importance rating (1–5), a cover photo, a gallery of photos or video, the people who were there, tags, and an era it belongs to. Dates don't have to be exact — mark one as approximate or *"era only"* if you only remember roughly when something happened.

### 📔 Journal
Longer-form writing, tied to the day you wrote it rather than a memory. Entries can be tagged with a mood, a place, the people involved, and attachments. Every entry defaults to **Private**; switching one to **Public** just means it's no longer PIN-gated (see *[Keeping some things more private](#-keeping-some-things-more-private-than-others)*, below) — there's still no public sharing feature anywhere in the app.

### ✍️ Writings
Essays, quotes, poems, letters, unsent messages, short stories, stray thoughts — anything you composed on purpose rather than logged as it happened. Same Public/Private treatment as journal entries.

### 🧑‍🤝‍🧑 People
Everyone who's shaped your life — family, friends, classmates, colleagues, romantic history, mentors, or your own custom categories. A profile can hold a photo, nickname, relationship status, date of birth, how and when you first met, a bio, and private notes only you'll see. Your own profile lives here too, marked as *"you,"* at the center of the connections graph.

### 🕸️ Connections
An interactive, draggable node graph of everything in the archive and how it relates — you at the center, with lines out to every person, memory, journal entry, and event you've archived, plus explicit relationships between people and inferred links between things that happened on the same day. Zoom, pan, and drag nodes to rearrange the layout; click any node to jump straight to it.

### 📍 Places
Anywhere that mattered enough to name — with a description, category, and an optional map location picker. Places connect automatically to any memory, journal entry, or event tied to them.

### 🎉 Events & Eras
**Events** are dated happenings with a category — birthday, graduation, trip, milestone, first meeting, anniversary, and so on. **Eras** are the chapters you divide your life into (*"College," "The gap year," "Before the move"*) — a name with a start and end date. Memories and events fall into whichever era's date range they land in, or you can pin one by hand when the dates overlap.

### 🚀 Projects
Anything you built or attempted — with a status (active, completed, paused, abandoned, or failed — nothing here gets hidden just because it didn't work out), a goal, what you learned, and why it ended if it did.

### 🎨 Interests
Things you're into now or used to be into, with when you discovered them and when you were last active in them.

### 🎯 Goals
Things you're working toward, with a status, a target date, and a progress percentage. Beyond the current snapshot, you can log dated **check-ins** on any goal to build a real progress history over time instead of just seeing today's number.

### 🪞 Insights & Reflections
Reflections are **append-only** by design: editing one never overwrites what you wrote before, it adds a new version and keeps every earlier one readable — a conversation with your past self, not a place that quietly rewrites history. This page also charts your logged moods over the last six months.

### 🖼️ Media
Every photo, video, audio clip, or document you've attached anywhere in the archive, browsable in one place.

## 🔎 Finding things

- **Search** — the magnifying glass in the top nav does a live keyword search across people, memories, journal entries, places, events, projects, and writings as you type.
- **Calendar** — lays out everything with a date across an actual month grid, with birthdays and anniversaries recurring automatically every year like a real calendar. Jump to any month or year directly instead of clicking through one at a time.
- **Timeline** — groups everything chronologically by era (or by year, for anything outside a defined era).
- **Ask** — the chat bubble in the corner is a built-in assistant that answers questions about *your own archive*, grounded only in what you've actually recorded — including things that don't share the exact words you searched for. It never reaches outside your archive for an answer, and it won't surface anything you've locked.

## 🔒 Keeping some things more private than others

Everything in this archive is already private to your account. For the handful of entries that feel heavier than the rest, there's a second layer:

1. Set a 6-digit PIN in **Settings → Sensitive content PIN**.
2. Mark any journal entry, writing, or person's profile as **locked**.
3. A locked entry's title, content, and photos are hidden — from you included — until you enter the PIN. It's also invisible to search, the Ask assistant, on-this-day callouts, birthday reminders, and the calendar.
4. Entering the PIN once unlocks *everything* locked for 30 minutes, not just the one entry — so reviewing several sensitive things in one sitting doesn't mean re-entering it constantly.

You can't lock anything until a PIN exists, on purpose — that stops you from locking yourself out of your own writing by accident.

## 📤 Getting your data out

Settings has three export options:

- **JSON** — a full dump of everything, meant for backups or moving your data elsewhere.
- **Markdown** — a readable file, organized by section.
- **Printable view** — turn it into a PDF straight from your browser's print dialog.

The printable view leaves out anything you've locked; the JSON and Markdown exports include it, since exporting your own complete archive while signed in is different from something else being able to read it.

You can also bring history *in* — **Settings → Import** accepts a CSV of journal entries or people, so you don't have to hand-type years of history.

<br>

## 🏠 Whose it is

There's no public sign-up, ever. If you ever want your data gone, **Settings → Danger zone** permanently deletes the account and everything in it — irreversibly, on purpose.

Right up until then, it's a private, growing record of one life, kept the way you'd actually want to look back on it.
