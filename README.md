# Archive

**A private, single-owner life archive.** A place to keep the people, places, memories, projects, and small moments that made you — searchable, connected, and never built to be seen by anyone but you.

This isn't a journaling app, a social network, or a photo backup tool, though it does pieces of all three. It's closer to a personal museum: every person you've known, every place that mattered, every memory worth keeping, every goal you chased or abandoned, every thing you made — recorded once, connected to everything else it touches, and kept private by default.

There is no sign-up, no feed, no followers, and no algorithm. The only account this archive will ever have is yours.

---

## The idea behind it

Most places you'd put this kind of thing — social media, a notes app, scattered photo albums — either weren't built to be private, weren't built to last, or weren't built to connect. A memory doesn't just have a date; it has people in it, a place it happened, and a chapter of your life it belongs to. This archive treats all of that as one connected graph instead of a pile of disconnected entries, so a person you add automatically becomes something you can trace through every memory, journal entry, and event they show up in.

Nothing here is designed to be performed for an audience. Journal entries are private by default. There's no "public profile." The one thing resembling a social feature — a connections graph — is centered entirely on you, because that's the only account that exists.

---

## Getting started

**First run.** The very first time the archive is opened, it asks you to create the one owner account it will ever have — your name, email, and a password. There's no invite system and no second account; this is a single-owner archive, permanently.

**Signing in.** After that, every visit starts at a sign-in screen. Your session stays open until you sign out.

**Setting the mood.** Head to **Settings → Atmospheres** early on and pick a visual theme — Minimal, Midnight, Cyber, Cute, Editorial, Archive, Retro/Pixel, Y2K, Organic, Futuristic, or Terminal — or build your own from scratch with the custom theme editor (every color, corner radius, and motion feel is adjustable). The theme you pick follows you everywhere, including the sign-in screen itself.

---

## Finding your way around

The navigation bar groups everything into four drawers:

- **Chronicle** — Timeline, Calendar, Events
- **People** — People, Places, Connections
- **Story** — Memories, Journal, Media
- **Made** — Projects, Interests, Goals, Writings, Insights

The home page gives you a snapshot the moment you sign in: an on-this-day callout for anything dated on today's day in a past year, upcoming birthdays and anniversaries in the next 30 days, a nudge if it's been a while since you last journaled, a wall of recently-added photos, and a "surprise me" button that pulls up something at random from anywhere in the archive.

## What you can keep here

### Memories
Titled, dated moments — the core unit of the archive. Each one can carry a description, a mood, an importance rating (1–5), a cover photo, a gallery of photos or video, the people who were there, tags, and an era it belongs to. Dates don't have to be exact — mark one as approximate or "era only" if you only remember roughly when something happened.

### Journal
Longer-form writing, tied to the day you wrote it rather than a memory. Journal entries can be tagged with a mood, a place, the people involved, and attachments. Every entry defaults to **Private**; switching one to **Public** just means it's no longer PIN-gated (see *Sensitive content*, below) — there's still no public sharing feature, everything in this archive is for your eyes only regardless of that setting.

### Writings
Essays, quotes, poems, letters, unsent messages, short stories, stray thoughts — anything you composed on purpose rather than logged as it happened. Same Public/Private treatment as journal entries.

### People
Everyone who's shaped your life — family, friends, classmates, colleagues, romantic history, mentors, or your own custom categories. A person's profile can hold a photo, nickname, relationship status, date of birth, how and when you first met, a public-ish bio, and private notes only you'll see. Your own profile lives here too, marked as "you," and is the center of the connections graph.

### Connections
An interactive, draggable node graph of everything in the archive and how it relates — you at the center, with lines out to every person, memory, journal entry, and event you've archived, plus explicit relationships between people (e.g. "sibling of," "college roommate of") and inferred links between things that happened on the same day. Zoom, pan, and drag nodes to rearrange the layout; click any node to jump straight to it.

### Places
Anywhere that mattered enough to name — with a description, category, and (if you want) a map location picker. Places connect automatically to any memory, journal entry, or event tied to them.

### Events & Eras
**Events** are dated happenings with a category — birthday, graduation, trip, milestone, first meeting, anniversary, and so on. **Eras** are the chapters you divide your life into (e.g. "College," "The gap year," "Before the move") — a name with a start and end date. Memories and events fall into whichever era's date range they land in, or you can pin one to a specific era by hand when the dates overlap.

### Projects
Anything you built or attempted — with a status (active, completed, paused, abandoned, or failed — nothing here gets hidden just because it didn't work out), a goal, what you learned, and why it ended if it did.

### Interests
Things you're into now or used to be into, with when you discovered them and when you were last active in them.

### Goals
Things you're working toward, with a status, a target date, and a progress percentage. Beyond the current snapshot, you can log dated **check-ins** on any goal to build a real progress history over time instead of just seeing today's number.

### Insights & Reflections
Reflections are append-only by design: editing one never overwrites what you wrote before, it adds a new version and keeps every earlier one readable. It's meant to be a conversation with your past self, not a place that quietly rewrites history. This page also charts your logged moods over the last six months, pulled from your memories and journal entries.

### Media
Every photo, video, audio clip, or document you've attached anywhere in the archive, browsable in one place.

## Finding things

- **Search** (the magnifying glass in the top nav) does a live keyword search across people, memories, journal entries, places, events, projects, and writings as you type.
- **Calendar** lays out everything with a date across an actual month grid — memories, journal entries, events, and project milestones on the day they happened, with birthdays and anniversaries recurring automatically every year the way a real calendar handles them. Jump to any month or year directly instead of clicking through one month at a time.
- **Timeline** groups everything chronologically by era (or by year, for anything outside a defined era).
- **Ask** (the chat bubble in the corner) is a built-in assistant that answers questions about your own archive — "when did I meet X," "what was I working on in 2023" — grounded only in what you've actually recorded, including things that don't share the exact words you're searching for. It never reaches outside your archive for an answer, and it won't surface anything you've locked (see below).

## Keeping some things more private than others

Everything in this archive is already private to your account. For the handful of entries that feel heavier than the rest, there's a second layer:

1. Set a 6-digit PIN in **Settings → Sensitive content PIN**.
2. Mark any journal entry, writing, or person's profile as **locked**.
3. A locked entry's title, content, and photos are hidden — from you included — until you enter the PIN. It's also invisible to search, the Ask assistant, on-this-day callouts, birthday reminders, and the calendar.
4. Entering the PIN once unlocks *everything* locked for 30 minutes, not just the one entry you unlocked — so if you're reviewing several sensitive things at once, you're not re-entering it constantly.

You can't lock anything until a PIN exists, on purpose — that stops you from locking yourself out of your own writing by accident.

---

## Getting your data out

Settings has three export options: a full **JSON** dump of everything (the format meant for backups or moving your data elsewhere), a **Markdown** file (readable, organized by section), and a **printable view** you can turn into a PDF straight from your browser's print dialog. The printable view leaves out anything you've locked; the JSON and Markdown exports include it, since exporting your own complete archive while signed in is different from something else being able to read it.

You can also bring history in — **Settings → Import** accepts a CSV of journal entries or people so you don't have to hand-type years of history.

---

## Whose it is

There's no public sign-up, ever. If you ever want your data gone, **Settings → Danger zone** permanently deletes the account and everything in it — irreversibly, on purpose. Right up until then, it's a private, growing record of one life, kept the way you'd actually want to look back on it.
